How hot will the greenhouse world be?

Open question

The answer depends on climate sensitivity to greenhouse gases, which models constrain only within a range.

What we know

  • Earth's average temperature has risen since the late 19th century. IPCC reports conclude that human influence, mainly through greenhouse gases, is the cause.
  • Atmospheric carbon dioxide has risen sharply since the industrial era, which is measured directly.
  • Projections depend on future emissions: the higher they are, the stronger the warming.

What we don't know

  • How far the climate will warm, since climate sensitivity to greenhouse gases is still known only within a range.
  • How much certain phenomena (clouds, ice, ocean currents) will amplify or dampen the warming.

Why it's hard

  • The climate is a system of many interacting elements, and we cannot run the experiment on a planetary scale.
  • Future emissions depend on human decisions, not only on physics.

Why it matters

Knowing what temperature will be reached makes it possible to decide what measures to take.

Where to start

  • Climatology and climate modelling.

Going further

Present-day climate change includes both global warming—the ongoing increase in global average temperature—and its wider effects on Earth's climate system. In a broader sense, climate change also includes previous long-term changes to Earth's climate. The modern-day rise in global temperatures is driven by human activities, especially fossil fuel burning since the Industrial Revolution. Fossil fuel use, deforestation, and some agricultural and industrial practices release greenhouse gases.
