Is the Hodge conjecture true?

Open question

It links the shape of certain geometric spaces to the algebraic equations that define them.

What we know

  • The conjecture links the topology of geometric objects defined by equations (algebraic varieties) to simpler algebraic objects.
  • It is proved in several special cases.
  • It is one of the seven Millennium Prize problems of the Clay Institute.

What we don't know

  • Whether it is true in general.
  • Whether a topological object of a certain type is always made of algebraic pieces.

Why it's hard

  • It connects tools from different areas, which requires new ideas.
  • There is no known general route to prove or refute it.

Why it matters

It would give a precise link between two ways of describing shapes, and advance algebraic geometry.

Going further

In mathematics, the Hodge conjecture is a major unsolved problem in algebraic geometry and complex geometry that relates the algebraic topology of a non-singular complex algebraic variety to its subvarieties.
