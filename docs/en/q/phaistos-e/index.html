What does the Phaistos disc say?

Documented mystery

A Cretan clay disc covered in spiral signs. It has never been deciphered.

What we know

  • It is a baked clay disc found in 1908 at Phaistos, in Crete. On both sides it carries signs arranged in a spiral, stamped with punches.
  • The disc is dated to about the second millennium BC. It was found with objects of the same period.
  • No reading has been accepted by specialists.

What we don't know

  • What the signs say, and even in which language.
  • Whether it is a text, a game or another kind of object.

Why it's hard

  • We have only one disc: that is too little text to test a reading.
  • There is no comparable text in this script.

Why it matters

A decipherment would add a language to the history of the ancient Mediterranean.

Where to start

  • Aegean archaeology and the study of undeciphered scripts.
  • A first step: compare its signs with those of Linear A.
  • See the Wikipedia page below.

Going further

The Phaistos Disc, or Phaistos Disk, is a disc of fired clay from the island of Crete, Greece, possibly from the middle or late Minoan Bronze Age, bearing a text in an unknown script and language. Its purpose and its original place of manufacture remain disputed. It is now on display at the archaeological museum of Heraklion. The name is sometimes spelled Phaestos or Festos.
