Can Linear A be deciphered?

Documented mystery

The script of Minoan Crete. The signs are known, the language is not.

What we know

  • Linear A is the script used in Crete in the Minoan period, in the second millennium BC. Its signs are known.
  • Some signs resemble those of Linear B, which records an early form of Greek and was deciphered in 1952. So part of the sound values can be read.
  • The language recorded is not identified, so the texts cannot be understood.

What we don't know

  • The language of Linear A: we do not know which family it belongs to.
  • The meaning of most texts, which are short lists on tablets.

Why it's hard

  • The texts found are short and few, often accounts.
  • With no bilingual text, no other known language provides a foothold.

Why it matters

It would make it possible to read the script of the oldest Bronze Age civilisation in Europe.

Going further

Linear A is a writing system that was used by the Minoans of Crete from 1800 BC to 1450 BC. Linear A was the primary script used in palace and religious writings of the Minoan civilization. It was adapted into Linear B, which was used by the Mycenaeans to write an early form of Greek. Since its modern rediscovery in 1900, no texts in the script have yet been deciphered.
