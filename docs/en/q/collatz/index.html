Does the Collatz rule always lead to 1?

Open question

Halve an even number; multiply an odd number by 3 and add 1; repeat. Every number tested by computer ends at 1, but nobody has proved it.

What we know

  • Rule: if the number is even, divide it by 2; if odd, multiply it by 3 and add 1. Repeat.
  • Every number tested by computer ends up reaching 1.
  • In 2019 Terence Tao proved that almost all numbers reach very small values, in a precise sense, without proving that they all reach 1.

What we don't know

  • Whether all numbers reach 1.
  • Whether some number has a sequence that grows without end, or a loop other than 4, 2, 1.

Why it's hard

  • The rule mixes multiplication and division, giving sequences with no apparent regularity.
  • There is no general method for studying this kind of sequence.

Why it matters

It is a problem easy to state that has resisted for a long time. It shows that an elementary rule can hide deep difficulty.

Where to start

  • Number theory and dynamical systems.
  • A first step: compute by hand the sequence for 27.
  • The article below is Tao's, on the behaviour of almost all orbits.

Going further

The Collatz conjecture is one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics. The conjecture asks whether repeating two simple arithmetic operations will eventually transform every positive integer into 1. It concerns sequences of integers in which each term is obtained from the previous term as follows: if a term is even, the next term is one half of it. If a term is odd, the next term is 3 times the previous term plus 1.
