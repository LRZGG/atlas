How far can human lifespan be extended?

Open question

Mice, worms and yeast live longer in the lab. Some researchers think human ageing can be slowed; others think our lifespan is more fixed.

What we know

  • Ageing is a gradual decline of the body's functions. Factors such as genome instability, telomere wear or inflammation contribute.
  • In laboratory animals, several interventions extend lifespan, such as calorie restriction.
  • In humans, average lifespan has increased a lot, but the maximum observed lifespan has stayed about the same.

What we don't know

  • Why we age: accumulated wear or a programmed process.
  • Whether there is a limit to human life, and what it is.

Why it's hard

  • Ageing is slow, which makes experiments in humans very long.
  • It combines many mechanisms that interact, so it is hard to isolate one.

Why it matters

Better understanding what causes ageing could delay age-related diseases, which are the main causes of death in rich countries.

Going further

Ageing is the process of becoming older. The term refers mainly to humans, many other animals, and fungi; whereas for example, bacteria, perennial plants and some simple animals are potentially biologically immortal. In a broader sense, ageing can refer to single cells within an organism which have ceased dividing, or to the population of a species.
