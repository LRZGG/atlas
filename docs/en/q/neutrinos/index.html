What is the mass of neutrinos?

Open question

We know neutrinos have a mass, but not its value. We also do not know whether they are their own antiparticle.

Draft written with an AI (Claude). The references exist, checked in OpenAlex; the text has not yet been reviewed by a specialist. Who writes what

What we know

  • Neutrinos are very light, very numerous particles that interact very little with matter.
  • In 1998 the Super-Kamiokande experiment showed that neutrinos change type as they travel (oscillation), which implies they have mass.
  • Mass differences between types are measured, but not the masses themselves.

What we don't know

  • The mass of each neutrino.
  • Whether they are their own antiparticle (Majorana neutrinos) or not.
  • The ordering of their masses (normal or inverted hierarchy).

Why it's hard

  • Their interaction is so weak that very large detectors are needed to see a few of them.
  • The mass is so small that it is hard to measure directly.

Why it matters

Their mass does not fit in the standard model: knowing it points to what lies beyond, and could explain why the universe contains more matter than antimatter.

Going further

A neutrino is an elementary particle that interacts with other matter only via the weak interaction and gravity. It is electrically neutral, and its mass is so small that it was long thought to be zero. Neutrinos usually pass through other matter with no detectable effect because the weak force has a very short range, and their gravitational interaction is extremely weak due to the tiny mass of the neutrino.
