What were the Nazca lines for?

Documented enigma

Large figures and lines drawn in the Peruvian desert. Their purpose is debated.

What we know

  • These are large figures and long lines drawn in the desert of southern Peru, by removing the dark stones from the ground to expose lighter soil.
  • They were made by the region's cultures, over several centuries. They are visible from afar, but are seen above all from the air.
  • The figures are very numerous and varied: animals, plants, geometric shapes, straight lines.

What we don't know

  • Their purpose: several hypotheses are proposed (ritual, tied to water, to astronomy), none prevails.
  • How the groups that drew them organised themselves to produce such regular figures.

Why it's hard

  • There is no text from these cultures explaining their role.
  • The very dry climate preserves them, which helps, but erosion and tourism threaten them.

Why it matters

They raise a general question: what can we conclude from the use of a place without writing?

Where to start

  • Landscape archaeology and the study of climate.
  • A first step: compare the layouts with the distribution of water sources in the region.
  • The Wikipedia page below gives the entry point.

Going further

The Nazca lines are a group of geoglyphs made in the soil of the Nazca Desert in southern Peru. They were created between 500 BC and 500 AD by people making depressions or shallow incisions in the desert floor, removing pebbles and leaving different-colored dirt exposed. There are two major phases of the Nazca lines, Paracas phase, from 400 to 200 BC, and Nazca phase, from 200 BC to 500 AD.
