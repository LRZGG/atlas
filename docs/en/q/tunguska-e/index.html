What exploded over Tunguska in 1908?

Documented enigma

An explosion flattened millions of trees in Siberia. It is most often attributed to an asteroid or comet breaking up in the air; details are debated.

What we know

  • On 30 June 1908 an explosion flattened trees over a very large area of forest in Siberia. There is no impact crater.
  • The most widespread explanation is the break-up in the air of a small stony asteroid or a comet.
  • Models of the atmospheric entry of a stony asteroid reproduce the observed blast effect.

What we don't know

  • The exact nature of the object (asteroid, comet, other) and its size.
  • The details of the explosion, since studies on the ground took place only years later.

Why it's hard

  • The event is old, in a remote area, and the first expeditions came long after. There are almost no direct measurements.
  • The lack of a crater forces reasoning from ground damage and from models.

Why it matters

Understanding this kind of event helps assess the risk posed to Earth by the impact of a small object, which remains more frequent than that of a large one.

Going further

The Tunguska event was a large explosion that occurred on the morning of 30 June [O.S. 17 June] 1908, at around 7:14 KRAT in Central Siberia, then part of the Russian Empire. The cataclysm occurred in a remote and sparsely populated region of the taiga, near the Podkamennaya Tunguska River after which it is named. The shock wave knocked several witnesses to the ground, killed at least three people as well as numerous animals, and shattered windows within a 200 km (120 mi) radius.
