How did the Antikythera mechanism work?

Documented mystery

An ancient geared mechanism, recovered from a wreck in 1901. Exactly how it worked is still being studied.

What we know

  • The mechanism was recovered in 1901 from a wreck near the island of Antikythera. It consists of many fragments of corroded bronze bearing toothed wheels.
  • X-ray tomography made it possible to read inscriptions and count the teeth of the gears. It was used to compute positions of celestial bodies and calendar cycles.
  • Several reconstructions have been proposed, including a 2021 model of the planetary dials.

What we don't know

  • What the complete machine looked like: a significant part of the pieces is missing.
  • Who made it and where this know-how came from: no other object of the same kind from that period has been found.

Why it's hard

  • The fragments are corroded and mixed up, and some were lost at sea.
  • A complete mechanism must be deduced from pieces, with no written plan: several reconstructions remain possible.

Why it matters

It shows that ancient astronomy was capable of precision mechanics thought to have appeared much later, and it raises the question of what was lost.

Going further

The Antikythera mechanism is an ancient Greek hand-powered orrery built in the 2nd century BC and discovered in 1901. It is the oldest known example of an analogue computer. It could be used to predict astronomical positions and eclipses decades in advance. It could also be used to track the four-year cycle of athletic games similar to an olympiad, the cycle of the ancient Olympic Games.
