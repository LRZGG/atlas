Where did the 1977 radio signal come from?

Documented enigma

The Big Ear radio telescope (Ohio) recorded a narrow, intense signal for 72 seconds. It has never been received again.

Draft written with an AI (Claude). The references exist, checked in OpenAlex; the text has not yet been reviewed by a specialist. Who writes what

What we know

  • On 15 August 1977 the Big Ear radio telescope (Ohio State University) recorded an intense, narrow-band radio signal near 1,420 MHz, the frequency of neutral hydrogen.
  • The signal lasted 72 seconds, the time the source took to cross the antenna's field. The astronomer Jerry Ehman circled the data on the printout and wrote “Wow!” in the margin.
  • Later searches, with more sensitive radio telescopes, have not found a signal of this type at the same place in the sky.

What we don't know

  • Where it came from: a natural phenomenon, terrestrial interference or something else. No explanation has consensus.
  • Whether it repeated: a single detection exists, so it cannot be checked by repetition.

Why it's hard

  • A one-off event cannot be measured twice: it cannot be reproduced or studied more closely.
  • The telescope was observing a fixed point in the sky: the direction of the source is known only to within a region, not a point.

Why it matters

It is a test of method: what do we do with a single signal that cannot be repeated? It also serves as a reference for searches for extraterrestrial signals.

Going further

The Wow! signal was a strong narrowband radio signal detected on August 15, 1977, by Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope in the United States, then used to support the search for extraterrestrial intelligence. The signal appeared to come from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius and bore expected hallmarks of extraterrestrial origin.
