Why did animal life diversify so fast in the Cambrian?

Open question

Almost all major animal groups appear in the fossil record over a geologically short period. The cause is debated.

What we know

  • Fossils of complex animals are rare before the Cambrian, about 540 million years ago. Cambrian rocks contain many, representing most of today's major animal groups.
  • Older organisms existed, such as those of the Ediacaran. The diversification spread over a few tens of millions of years.
  • Several explanations are proposed: more oxygen, the appearance of predators, new developmental genes, environmental changes.

What we don't know

  • Which cause, or set of causes, triggered this diversification.
  • How far the “explosion” is real, or due to gaps in the fossil record.

Why it's hard

  • Fossils from that time are rare and unevenly preserved: only hard-bodied animals or those from exceptional sites are included.
  • The possible causes are many, linked to one another (ocean chemistry, ecology, genetics) and hard to separate.

Why it matters

Understanding how the great diversity of animals arose, and what makes rapid evolutionary change possible.

Where to start

  • Palaeontology and developmental biology.
  • A first step: compare the faunas of the Ediacaran and the Cambrian.
  • The two articles below review the hypotheses.

Going further

The Cambrian explosion is a major evolutionary radiation event of the Earth's lifeforms beginning approximately 538.8 million years ago in the early Cambrian period of the Paleozoic era, during which a sudden diversification of complex life occurred in the fossil record.
