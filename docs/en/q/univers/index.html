What is the universe made of?

Open question

Ordinary matter, the stuff of stars and galaxies, makes up less than 5% of what exists. The nature of the rest is unknown.

Draft written with an AI (Claude). The references exist, checked in OpenAlex; the text has not yet been reviewed by a specialist. Who writes what

What we know

  • The movements of galaxies and galaxy clusters indicate there is more mass than what we see. It is called dark matter.
  • Measurements of the cosmic microwave background give a breakdown of the universe's energy: ordinary matter is only a small part, dark matter about five times more.
  • Very little is known about its nature: many candidates have been proposed and tested, with no direct detection so far.

What we don't know

  • The nature of dark matter: an unknown particle, dark objects, or a modification of gravity.
  • The nature of dark energy, which accounts for the accelerating expansion of the universe.

Why it's hard

  • Dark matter neither emits nor receives light: it is detected only through its gravity.
  • Detectors are very hard to build, since the expected signal is extremely faint.

Why it matters

Knowing what most of the universe's matter is made of, and whether the laws of gravity are complete.

Going further

In astronomy and cosmology, dark matter is an invisible and hypothetical form of matter that does not interact with electromagnetic radiation, including light. Dark matter is implied by gravitational effects that cannot be explained by general relativity unless more matter is present than can be observed.
