Do the Navier–Stokes equations always have a smooth solution?

Solution proposed, under evaluation

These equations have described fluid flow since the 19th century. The problem asks whether their solutions always exist and stay smooth in three dimensions.

Navier–Stokes: a singularity announced by OpenAI

Announced, not accepted · 2026-09-08

On 8 September 2026 OpenAI announced that a group of AI agents, running on a model that is not public, had found a singularity in the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations. According to Quanta Magazine, the result has been checked in the Lean proof language; humans still have to check that the Lean statement matches what was meant to be proved. This is not an accepted result yet: the Clay Institute still lists the problem as “Active” and has awarded nothing (consulted November 2026), priority is disputed, and we found no peer review. Earlier, in September 2025, a DeepMind-led team used neural networks to find unstable singularities in other equations (porous media, Euler with a boundary), not in Navier–Stokes.

What we know

  • The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of fluids such as water and air. They are used in meteorology, aeronautics and many other fields.
  • In two dimensions, it can be proved that solutions exist and stay smooth.
  • In three dimensions, existence of solutions is proved for a short time, or weaker solutions, but not for all time with smoothness.

What we don't know

  • Whether, in three dimensions, solutions always stay smooth, or whether a singularity can appear.
  • What kind of mathematics could answer it.

Why it's hard

  • The equations are nonlinear: large-scale and small-scale effects get mixed.
  • Turbulence makes the behaviour of solutions hard to describe.

Why it matters

The Clay Institute offers a million dollars to whoever answers. It is also a basic question for understanding turbulence.

Going further

The question of whether the Navier–Stokes equations always have smooth solutions in three-dimensional Euclidean space, given some initial conditions and possibly an external force, has been a longstanding unsolved problem in mathematics since the early 20th century. The equations are a system of partial differential equations that describe the motion of a fluid in space.
